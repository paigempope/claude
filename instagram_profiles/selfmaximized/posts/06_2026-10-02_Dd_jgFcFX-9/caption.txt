IMPERMANENCE IS THE ONLY CONSTANT. HERE IS HOW TO MAKE PEACE WITH IT:
Everything you are holding right now, the season you are in, the version of yourself you have become, the life that feels either settled or uncertain, is temporary. Not as a threat. As a fact. And one of the most liberating things a human being can do is stop fighting that fact and start moving with it.

Impermanence is the fundamental nature of existence. Every cell in the body is replaced over time. Every relationship evolves. Every season, literal and metaphorical, gives way to the next. Heraclitus observed that you cannot step into the same river twice. Modern neuroscience confirms the same truth from a different angle. The brain is never the same brain it was yesterday. Neuroplasticity ensures that every experience, every loss and every joy, is physically reshaping the organ that processes your reality.

The resistance to impermanence is also well documented. The brain’s default is to predict and maintain. Novelty and uncertainty activate the same threat circuitry as physical danger. This is why change, even positive change, can feel destabilizing. The nervous system does not distinguish between a threat to survival and a threat to the familiar. Both register as something to brace against.

Research on psychological flexibility, the capacity to adapt without losing oneself, consistently shows it is one of the strongest predictors of mental health, resilience, and life satisfaction. The people who suffer least through change are not the ones who experience less of it. They are the ones who have stopped requiring life to stay still.

Understanding the brevity of life sharpens this further. When we truly absorb that time is finite and every moment unrepeatable, the grip on how things should be loosens. What remains is the only thing that was ever real. What is here now.

Impermanence is not something happening to you. It is something you are made of.
