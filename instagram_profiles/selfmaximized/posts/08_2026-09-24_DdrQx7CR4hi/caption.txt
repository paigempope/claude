WHAT SUPPRESSING YOUR EMOTIONS IS DOING TO YOUR BODY:
We live in a culture that rewards composure. Keeping it together. Moving on. Being fine. And while emotional regulation is a genuine skill, there is a significant difference between processing an emotion and suppressing one. The research on what happens when we chronically choose the latter is one of the most important and overlooked stories in health science.

Emotional suppression is not neutrality. It is active work. Studies using physiological monitoring show that when people suppress an emotional response, their subjective experience of the emotion does not decrease but their body’s stress response increases. The feeling stays. The body just absorbs it alone. Cortisol rises. Heart rate elevates. The nervous system remains activated while the face stays composed.

Research by James Gross on emotion regulation shows that habitual suppression is associated with higher rates of depression, anxiety, cardiovascular disease, and impaired immune function. People who regularly suppress emotions also report lower relationship satisfaction and social connection, because authentic connection requires emotional authenticity. You cannot selectively numb. When you suppress the difficult, you dull the beautiful too.

The body stores what the mind refuses to feel. Van der Kolk’s research on trauma shows that unexpressed emotion does not disappear. It lives in the body as chronic muscle tension, disrupted digestion, hormonal dysregulation, and a nervous system that cannot fully settle. The body keeps the score whether we are paying attention or not.

Feeling is not weakness. It is the body’s most efficient processing system. Grief felt moves through. Grief suppressed calcifies. Anger expressed appropriately releases. Anger swallowed becomes inflammation.

The most radical act of self care is sometimes simply allowing yourself to feel what is already there.
