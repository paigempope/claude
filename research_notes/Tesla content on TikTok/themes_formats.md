# Tesla Content Themes and Video Formats on TikTok: What Performs (2024–Oct 2026)

Method note: About 17 search/fetch calls. TikTok's own pages (discover/tag pages) don't expose reliable per-video view counts to fetch tools, so nearly all numbers below are snapshots that news outlets reported at publication time. Treat them as lower bounds. No source gives a systematic, data-driven ranking of Tesla TikTok themes (for example, a social-listening dataset). The ranking below is therefore an inference from documented viral examples, not a measured result.

## Which themes reliably generate the most views, and which generate the most comments and shares?

### Takeaway
The best-documented view drivers are mockery or reaction content made by non-owners, especially about the Cybertruck (millions to tens of millions of views), Optimus robot clips (2M+ on TikTok, tens of millions across platforms), and anti-Musk or "ByeTesla" political content from 2025. Owner-utility content (hidden features, tips) reliably reaches mid-six figures to about 1M. Sentry Mode vandalism or "caught" clips sit around 1M. Political and Cybertruck content draws the most comments, which are polarized and hostile. Documented view counts for Robotaxi and FSD content are much smaller on TikTok.

### Cited Findings

It took a Cybertruck influencer's (TechieDani) "family-friendly features" claims and showed that a Toyota Sequoia matches each one. The dealership's social manager said the TikTok comments were "almost entirely anti-Cybertruck" while responses on X differed. — [Yahoo Autos / Jalopnik syndication](https://autos.yahoo.com/toyota-dealerships-viral-tiktok-roasts-164200893.html)

It had 2.9M+ views (Feb 2024). Comments mocked the truck as "just a truck," compared it to a Roblox vehicle or a child's drawing, and called seeing one a "jumpscare." — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/tiktok/woman-reveals-unfortunate-side-effect-of-owning-a-tesla-cybertruck-2518047/); [Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/news/what-its-like-to-own-cybertruck/)

- Spam accounts paste AI-generated Cybertruck "copypasta" into the captions of unrelated videos so the algorithm classes the posts as informational. That suggests the Cybertruck keyword itself was seen as a reach booster (2024). — [Daily Dot](https://lede-v2.dailydot.com/tesla-cybertruck-ai-copypasta)

### Inferences
- **Ranking by documented views (high to low):** (1) Cybertruck mockery/reaction and brand-vs-Cybertruck comparisons; (2) Optimus robot oddity clips; (3) political/anti-Musk content (ByeTesla, sticker comedy, Cybertruck hunters); (4) Sentry Mode "creepy" or vandalism captures (about 1M); (5) hidden features and tips (about 0.9M); (6) cost-of-ownership shock (about 0.4M); (7) Robotaxi and FSD (likes in the tens of thousands, with higher reach mainly from outlets like Paris Match and CNET).
- On TikTok, the content that travels furthest is often made by non-owners or critics about Tesla, not by owners for owners. TikTok's audience skews younger and less pro-Musk than X's. The Toyota dealer's report of "almost entirely anti-Cybertruck" TikTok comments versus different reactions on X supports this.
- Comments are driven by polarization. Cybertruck, Optimus ("is it remote controlled?"), Sentry Mode privacy, and politics all trigger arguments. Shares are probably driven by utility (tips) and spectacle (robots, light shows).
- **Under-documented categories:** Model Y Juniper, Model 3 Highland and Standard model reveals; delivery day; road trips, camping and charging; mods, wraps and TikTok Shop accessories; and comparisons with BYD, Rivian or Xiaomi. They likely perform steadily inside the Tesla-owner niche ("#teslatok") but did not generate press-covered viral breakouts that I could find.

### Gaps
- I could not find TikTok view counts for FSD fail clips, Cybertruck rust or recall videos, Juniper or Standard model content, delivery-day videos, TikTok Shop accessory videos, or EV comparison videos (Xiaomi SU7, BYD, Rivian). The SU7-vs-Model 3 coverage I found was about specs and sales, not TikTok performance ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/chinese-vs-american-ev-xioamis-162020089.html)).
- No Tesla-specific TikTok analytics report exists in my results. General benchmark: TikTok delivered nearly double Instagram's median engagement rate in 2025 across brands ([Emplifi](https://go.emplifi.io/rs/284-ENW-442/images/Emplifi-Social-Media-Benchmarks-Report.pdf)).
- I could not verify which outlet reported a "man beating a parked Tesla with a bat" TikTok by @adidasemoji at 1.4M views. The search snippet mentioned it, but I could not attribute it, so it is excluded from the findings.

## What formats work: POV, Sentry Mode compilations, tutorial/how-to, reaction, "things I wish I knew", day-in-the-life, skits, ASMR, before/after, green-screen commentary?

### Takeaway
The formats behind the documented hits are: duet/stitch-style comparison or reaction (Toyota dealer); before/after transitions set to a trending sound (#ByeTesla); one-tip tutorials ("hidden feature you didn't know"); found-footage POV (Sentry Mode, robot interactions); owner-diary or day-in-the-life clips (Cybertruck attention); genre-mashup skits (GRWM while FSD drives); and staged or prank skits (dumpster, Vision Pro, fake airbag). I found no evidence on ASMR or green-screen commentary for Tesla.

### Cited Findings
- **Duet/side-by-side reaction:** Toyota of Hattiesburg's deadpan cuts responded to a Cybertruck influencer's claims point by point. It drew tens of millions of cross-platform views, and the manager said it was inspired by similar Facebook clips. — [Yahoo Autos](https://autos.yahoo.com/toyota-dealerships-viral-tiktok-roasts-164200893.html)
- **Before/after transition with a trending sound:** #ByeTesla videos show the Tesla, then the replacement car, set to Taylor Swift's "Look What You Made Me Do" (spring 2025). — [FOX 11 LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/bye-tesla-tik-tok-trend)
- **Single-tip tutorial:** The NJ dealer's hidden-features clip (Green Traffic Light Chime) reached 900K+ views. — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/770910/new-jersey-tesla-hidden-features/)
- **Day-in-the-life / owner diary:** Abner's "everywhere you go, people want to know about this truck" clip reached 2.9M views. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/tiktok/woman-reveals-unfortunate-side-effect-of-owning-a-tesla-cybertruck-2518047/)
- **Genre mashup:** a GRWM routine filmed while FSD drives the car. — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/768540/tesla-grwm-fsd-autopilot-houston/)
- **Found footage / POV:** Sentry Mode recordings (1.1M–1.2M examples) and robot "personal space" POV (2.4M). — [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/795079/tesla-sentry-mode/); [Briefly](https://briefly.co.za/people/203509-video-tesla-optimus-robot-personal-space-has)
- **Comedy sketch from a media brand:** The Daily Show posted a TikTok, "Can't sell your Tesla? Here's a solution: anti-Elon stickers," with Ricky Velez (March 2025). — [TikTok @thedailyshow](https://www.tiktok.com/@thedailyshow/video/7479520242973330734?lang=en)
- **Staged content risk:** A TikToker's viral "faulty Tesla airbag" video was fake. Viewers noticed the car was still in Transport Mode, and he publicly apologized to Musk. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-viral-video-fake-hoax-elon-musk-apology-tesla-faulty-1605619)
- **Light-show performance clips** pair the car with music for spectacle. One reached 4.2M (2022, older data). — [iTech Post](https://www.itechpost.com/articles/108547/20220103/epic-tesla-light-show-tiktok-video-goes-viral-elon-musk.htm)

### Inferences
- **Hook archetypes that recur:** "you didn't know your Tesla could…", "the car was recording me…", "everyone stops me because of this truck", "look what you made me do." These are open-loop curiosity or outrage hooks within the first second.
- **Borrowing an audience:** formats that respond to someone else's content (duet/stitch with an influencer, a dealer responding to an owner) outperform formats that only target the owner niche.
- Staged or fake clips can go viral but invite debunking backlash, and pro-Tesla communities actively scrutinize them (the Transport Mode tell).

### Gaps
- I found no documented examples of Tesla ASMR (for example, frunk or door sounds), green-screen commentary on Tesla news, or "things I wish I knew before buying a Tesla" videos with view counts.
- No source compares format performance side by side for the same theme.

## How did the 2025 Musk/DOGE backlash and Tesla Takedown protests change what Tesla content performs?

### Takeaway
In 2025 political content became a major Tesla genre on TikTok: #ByeTesla trade-in videos, "selling the Nazi mobile" posts, anti-Elon bumper-sticker comedy, Cybertruck tailgate projections, and Sentry Mode clips of politically motivated vandalism. Tesla's own response was "edgy" posting concentrated on X rather than TikTok, which left TikTok's Tesla conversation largely to critics and owners.

### Cited Findings
- #ByeTesla (April 2025) emerged alongside the Tesla Takedown protests against Musk's DOGE role. Edmunds reported record Tesla trade-ins in March 2025. Some owners interviewed rejected politicizing their cars ("I just drive it as a car"), and one blamed left-wing rhetoric for the vandalism. — [FOX 11 LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/bye-tesla-tik-tok-trend)
- Owners offloaded cars after Musk's salute controversy ("I'm selling the Nazi mobile"), and the "I bought this before Elon went crazy" bumper-sticker trend took off. — [AOL/Guardian syndication](https://www.aol.com/finance/m-selling-nazi-mobile-tesla-130009427.html)
- Coverage also reported trading Teslas for other EVs to the Taylor Swift sound as "the hot new TikTok trend." — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/hot-tiktok-trend-trading-teslas-194800879.html)
- The Daily Show's anti-Elon sticker sketch on TikTok (March 2025). — [TikTok @thedailyshow](https://www.tiktok.com/@thedailyshow/video/7479520242973330734?lang=en)
- An anti-Musk "Don't buy a Swasticar" spoof Tesla ad went viral (Feb 2025). — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/culture/2025/02/27/dont-buy-a-swasticar-anti-elon-musk-tesla-advert-goes-viral)
- @cybertruck_hunters projected anti-Musk messages, reaching 1.1M+ likes by March 2025. — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/culture/cybertruck-hunters-projections/)
- Sentry Mode vandalism clips became news hooks tied to "attacks on company, Elon Musk grow nationwide." — [CBS Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/texas/news/viral-video-shows-man-keying-tesla-in-dallas-as-attacks-on-company-elon-musk-grow-nationwide)
- Tesla's own response was "edgy," joke-style posts mostly on X (it grew from 13 to 37 affiliated accounts). Electrek argued this "preaches to the choir" and that Tesla should be on Instagram and TikTok instead. — [Electrek, Apr 10 2025](https://electrek.co/2025/04/10/tesla-edgy-social-media-marketing-amid-brand-damage/); also [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tesla-social-media-ads/)
- Tesla's brand value fell $15.4B (about 36%) in 2025 to $27.61B, its third straight decline, per Brand Finance. — [CNBC, Jan 27 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/27/tesla-brand-value-2025-musk-politics.html)
- A counter-current, the TikToker's fake airbag video, ended with a public apology to Musk. It shows pro-Tesla audiences policing anti-Tesla virality. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-viral-video-fake-hoax-elon-musk-apology-tesla-faulty-1605619)

### Inferences
- The backlash turned Tesla into an identity-signaling topic on TikTok. "Selling my Tesla," before/after trade-ins, and sticker or owner-shame comedy gained an outsized audience among non-owners, while neutral owner-utility content kept serving the niche.
- Rage-bait in both directions (anti-Musk mockery; "they keyed my car" victimhood) drives comments. Sentry Mode clips gained a political frame they lacked before 2025.
- Pro-Tesla creators increasingly framed content as "it's just a car / I don't care about the CEO." That is an inference from the owner quotes in FOX 11, not a measured trend.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (for example, hashtag view totals for #ByeTesla, #TeslaTakedown, or #ElonWentCrazy) was retrievable.
- I found nothing on whether political Tesla content cooled after Musk left DOGE (mid-2025) or into 2026.
- I found no data on resale-value-drop videos as a TikTok genre.

## What Tesla content flops or gets suppressed?

### Takeaway
I found evidence of three things. Tesla's own corporate "edgy" humor is widely seen as falling flat, and Tesla largely isn't on TikTok. Fabricated or staged failure videos get debunked and backfire. Keyword-stuffing Cybertruck spam exists, which suggests TikTok reach games around the topic. I found no reliable evidence that TikTok specifically suppresses Tesla content.

### Cited Findings
- Tesla's edgy posts (Diamond Black color meme, the "ASS" Smart Summon joke, the dire-wolf turn-signal joke) were judged by Electrek to "fall flat," and Tesla's X focus limits its TikTok presence. — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/04/10/tesla-edgy-social-media-marketing-amid-brand-damage/)
- The fake airbag TikTok was exposed (Transport Mode tell), forcing an apology. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/tiktok-viral-video-fake-hoax-elon-musk-apology-tesla-faulty-1605619)
- Staged virality precedent: the 2022 "dog driving a Tesla" video was staged but still drew 10M+ views on Reddit (older data). — [Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/?p=39006); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/culture/please-dont-let-your-dog-drive-your-tesla-please)
- Spam accounts paste AI Cybertruck text into captions to game TikTok and Instagram categorization. — [Daily Dot](https://lede-v2.dailydot.com/tesla-cybertruck-ai-copypasta)
- Platform-adjacent risk to FSD hands-off and inattentive-driving clips: German coverage flags their legality and danger (Aug 2026), which raises possible policy (dangerous-acts) exposure. — [Elektroquatsch](https://www.elektroquatsch.de/artikel/tesla-fsd-hands-off-videos-gefaehrlich-ueberwachung-legalitaet)
- Musk previously complained that FSD beta testers ignored an NDA and shared lots of videos (older data, pre-2024). — [Business Insider NL](https://businessinsider.nl/elon-musk-says-tesla-owners-dont-seem-to-listen-to-me-because-they-ignore-an-nda-and-share-lots-of-videos-of-the-companys-full-self-driving-tech/)
- The Robotaxi ride-along TikToks I found showed modest engagement (about 9.5K–73.7K likes for creator posts). — [TikTok discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/tesla-robotaxi-austin)

### Inferences
- **Likely underperformers relative to effort:** spec-heavy reviews, earnings or stock commentary, and corporate-style Tesla promos. The lack of viral examples supports this, though it is not measured. Robotaxi rides look like a novelty that peaked at launch and then performed modestly on TikTok compared with X and YouTube, where Tesla-fan creators concentrate.
- FSD "hands-off" or sleeping-driver clips could fall under TikTok's dangerous-activities rules. I found no documented takedowns.

### Gaps
- I found no documented TikTok removals, shadowbans, or "suppression" of Tesla content, either pro or anti, and no creator testimony on it from Reddit (r/TeslaMotors, r/TeslaLounge). Reddit searches were not completed within the call budget.
- I did not establish whether TikTok deprioritizes political protest content about Tesla.
